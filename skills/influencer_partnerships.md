# Influencer and Creator Partnerships (Spain and France)

How to propose a collaboration to a fashion creator, and how to answer her.
Written for a small fashion brand whose customers are women aged 35 to 65,
but the rules hold for any brand writing to creators. Used by the Writer and
the Handler when the person is a creator rather than a business buyer.

## Who is a good fit

- Her audience matches the customer: women 35-65 who dress for work,
  events and everyday life. Her age matters less than her audience's.
- She already shows clothes she wears herself, with her own styling and
  her own words. Not only hauls, not only giveaways.
- Engagement looks human: comments that are real sentences, replies from
  her, steady numbers. Sudden follower jumps, comments that are all emojis
  or the same three words, or a tiny engagement rate on a big account are
  red flags. Never contact on follower count alone.
- Nano (1k-10k) and micro (10k-100k) creators convert better per euro and
  answer their own email. Start there.
- She publishes a professional contact for collaborations (email in bio,
  "colaboraciones"/"collaborations" on her website, a media kit form). Use
  only that channel. Never a personal address found elsewhere, never a
  family member, never a DM to a private account.

## What the first message must do

One person, one message, written for her. Never a template with her name
swapped in.

1. Open with something specific and true from her own content: a look, a
   video, a way she styles a piece. Only what the research notes say.
2. Say in one sentence who you are and why you are writing to her.
3. Make one concrete, honest proposal: try a piece, with no obligation to
   post. Conditions (fee, commission, code) come when she answers.
4. Ask one simple question she can answer in one line.
5. No links in the first message, no attachments, no discount codes, no
   "exclusive", no urgency.

Under 90 words. Spanish from Spain uses "tú" with creators unless she
writes "usted". French always "vous".

## What never to do

- Never offer payment for a post that hides the advertising. Any content
  in exchange for a gift, money or commission must be labelled as
  advertising: in Spain "Publicidad" or "#publi" visible from the start
  (Ley 13/2022 General de Comunicación Audiovisual, Real Decreto 444/2024
  for "usuarios de especial relevancia", and the Autocontrol code on
  influencers); in France "Publicité" or "Collaboration commerciale"
  (loi n° 2023-451 on influence commerciale). Say this plainly if she
  asks, and put it in any agreement.
- Never ask her to claim results she hasn't had ("this dress made me look
  ten years younger"), to use before/after images, or to make health or
  slimming claims about shapewear.
- Never promise a fee, a commission rate or a long-term deal that the
  product offer doesn't state. If she asks for conditions that aren't in
  the offer, say a person will answer and stop.
- Never follow up more than once. If she doesn't answer a single follow-up,
  she has answered.
- Never write to a minor or to an account that seems to be one.

## Handling her replies

- **Interested**: thank her, ask for her size and a postal address only
  for sending the piece, and say a person will confirm the details. The
  address is used to ship and nothing else.
- **Asks for her rates or sends a media kit**: thank her, say the team
  will review it and reply with a concrete proposal. Never negotiate money
  automatically: hand it to a human.
- **Asks how labelling works**: explain the content must be marked as
  advertising ("Publicidad"/"#publi" in Spain, "Publicité"/"Collaboration
  commerciale" in France) and that the brand will say so in the agreement.
- **Not now / not my style**: thank her warmly, don't argue, don't write
  again.
- **Any opt-out**: stop at once. Never answer an opt-out except to confirm
  it if a human decides to.

## What to measure

Per creator: reply (yes/no), accepted (yes/no), pieces sent and their cost,
posts published and labelled correctly, sessions and orders from her code or
link, revenue, and cost per order. A creator whose code brings no orders
after two posts is not renewed, however large her audience.
